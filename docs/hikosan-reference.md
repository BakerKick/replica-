# Hikosan Jingū — build reference

Facts gathered for the scroll-driven ascent. Sources at the bottom. Anything
marked ⚠︎ contradicts the draft this was based on, and the draft was wrong.

## The ascent

| | |
|---|---|
| Steps, bronze torii → Hōheiden | **824** ⚠︎ (draft said 800) |
| Distance | ~500 m of stone stair |
| Lower section | Houses and shukubō, **cherry-lined** ⚠︎ (draft had cedar throughout) |
| Upper section | Cedar grove (杉木立) |
| Both sides, whole length | **坊跡 — terraced stone retaining walls**, ruins of the yamabushi lodgings ⚠︎ (absent from the draft; the most distinctive feature of the real sandō) |
| Also on the path | Stone 二の鳥居, komainu, hōkyōintō stone pagodas, a 神橋 sacred bridge |

## 銅の鳥居 — the bronze torii (Important Cultural Property)

- Cast **1637** (Kan'ei 14)
- Donated by **Nabeshima Katsushige**, lord of Hizen/Saga ⚠︎ (draft credited Hosokawa Tadatoshi)
- **7 m tall**; pillar circumference **just over 3 m** — very thick columns
- Bronze, built from **stacked cylinder sections** — the seams are visible
- The 「英彦山」 plaque was granted by **Emperor Reigen in 1734** ⚠︎ — a century after the casting, not part of it

## 奉幣殿 — the Hōheiden (Important Cultural Property)

Formerly the great lecture hall (大講堂) of Reisen-ji, the centre of Hikosan
Shugendō. Predecessor burnt in the Ōtomo attack of 1568.

- Rebuilt **1616** (Genna 2) by **Hosokawa Tadaoki**, lord of Kokura
- **桁行 7 bays × 梁間 5 bays**, single storey
- **入母屋造** hip-and-gable, with a **1-bay 向拝** prayer porch
- Originally **寄棟造** hipped; converted to irimoya in **1723** by Ogasawara Tadataka
- **柿葺 kokerabuki** — thin **sawara cypress** shingle roof ⚠︎ (draft used tile).
  Steep pitch with a pronounced **反り** upward curve
- **朱塗り vermilion** columns and beams — thick, and **deliberately sparse in
  ornament**: read as weighty and clean, not gaudy
- Wraparound veranda with a large floor deck and **no handrail**
- **~33 m across the front and ~33 m deep** (roof plan, eaves included),
  **~16 m tall** ⚠︎ — far larger than the draft's 17 × 11
